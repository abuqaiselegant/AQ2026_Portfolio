A stock market prediction model for NSE NIFTY50 built on LASSO Regression, combining
technical indicators with time-lag features. Test RMSE of 14.78 and MAPE of 2.98% on
held-out data, with the methodology peer-reviewed and published in IEEE.

## The problem

Price prediction on a limited dataset invites overfitting. Technical analysis offers
dozens of candidate indicators, and it is easy to engineer more features than you have
meaningful observations to support — at which point the model memorises noise and
evaluates beautifully in-sample while failing on anything held out.

The objective was a model that stays honest on a limited initial dataset: enough features
to capture real sequential structure, with something forcing the useless ones out.

## Approach

The three indicators capture different things, which is why all three are present.
**Moving averages** smooth short-term noise to expose trend. **RSI** measures momentum,
flagging when recent movement is stretched relative to its own history. **Super Trend**
tracks trend direction with volatility taken into account. **Time-lag features** carry
prior values forward, which is what lets a regression model see sequence at all — without
them, each observation would be treated as independent, discarding the ordering that
matters most in price data.

**LASSO** is the deliberate choice, and it does two jobs at once. Its L1 penalty shrinks
coefficients toward zero and drives the genuinely uninformative ones *to* zero, so it
performs feature selection as part of fitting rather than as a separate step. On a
limited dataset with many correlated technical indicators, that is exactly the right
property — it keeps the model from spending its capacity on redundant features, and the
surviving non-zero coefficients say which indicators actually carried signal.

## Results

- **Test RMSE 14.78** on held-out NIFTY50 data
- **MAPE 2.98%**
- Methodology peer-reviewed and **published in IEEE**
